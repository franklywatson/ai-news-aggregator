New TasteVal Benchmark Shows AI 'Research Taste' Doubling Every 3 Months

P-Zero Research's TasteVal benchmark measures how well frontier models design experiments and interpret results across eight AI development tasks, benchmarked against experts from OpenAI, DeepMind, and NVIDIA. Opus 5.5 already exceeds the human baseline at 1/30th the cost per attempt, with research taste doubling every 3.0 months since December 2025. The result has led analysts to pull forward full recursive self-improvement timelines to 2027.

"Research taste is a key variable for recursive self-improvement — if models can design and interpret their own experiments better than human researchers, AI-driven AI progress accelerates compounding."

Rapid AI Progress Is Coming — Just Not Toward Superintelligence, Argues Leading Analyst

Nathan Lambert argues that AI agents will soon make training and inference engineering nearly autonomous, driving an exponential decline in the effective cost of model intelligence — but this won't fundamentally change the nature of the models themselves. He predicts pretraining research automation within 2-3 years and a Jevons-paradox surge in demand for agent products like Meta's Muse.

"This frames 'RSI' as grounded, parallelized AI-assisted engineering rather than takeoff — a plausible middle path with major implications for cost curves and agent product strategy."

Developer Tools & Infrastructure

Cache-2-Cache Lets Multi-Model Agents Hand Off Work Through KV Caches, Not Text

A new technique called Cache-2-Cache enables AI models in multi-agent pipelines to transfer context via key-value caches rather than regenerating text, eliminating compute waste at every handoff. Reported speedups reach 25x for multi-model agent orchestration.

"Context handoffs between specialized models are the dominant cost in multi-agent systems — direct KV cache transfer could reshape how agent orchestration layers are architected."

Cloudflare Acquires Deno, Betting on Durable-Object-Style Server Abstractions

Cloudflare is acquiring Deno to build on celld, its open-source implementation of the Durable Objects pattern, making self-hosted workerd a first-class runtime. The Deno runtime itself will only receive a year of maintenance before development ends, with creator Ryan Dahl saying it was 'sucked into the gravity well of node compatibility.'

"Stateful, object-storage-backed execution models are becoming a default substrate for server-side and agent workloads — and Deno's permission system was a notable sandboxing pattern for running untrusted AI-generated code."

Study: AI Coding Agents Produce More Code, Not More Shipped Software

"As engineering teams reorganize around AI coding agents, review capacity — not code generation — becomes the constraint, shifting investment toward verification and observability."
