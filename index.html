AI Models & Research

Google Ships Gemini 4 Neon: Frontier Agent Performance at Half the Cost

Google DeepMind released Gemini 4 Neon, its fourth Gemini 4 model in seven weeks, scoring 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and debuting #2 on the new Agent Arena Index. Neon offers a 1M-token context window, four effort levels, and is explicitly designed to sustain long-running, tool-heavy agent workflows — including orchestrating parallel Hydrogen subagents in Google Antigravity. At $3/$15 per million tokens, it costs 32% less per task than Carbon.

"Neon's agent-first design — reliable context compaction, low fabrication rates, and orchestration of subagents — shows frontier labs optimizing specifically for harness and agent workloads, not just raw benchmark scores."

Tencent's Open-Weights Hy4 Goes Multi-Modal and Live on Together AI

Tencent's 770B-parameter (49B active) Hy4 MoE model is now available on Together Serverless Inference, adding image input and computer-use capabilities including training on operating Blender and CAD tools. It carries a 1M-token context window and costs $0.83/$2.50 per million input/output tokens.

"Another open-weights frontier-class model with multimodal and computer-use abilities narrows the gap with proprietary offerings at a fraction of the price — and signals Chinese labs targeting professional-software agents specifically."

Self-Improvement Memory Lets Agent Harnesses Keep Learning Across Models and Benchmarks

A new paper introduces Continual Recursive Self-Improvement (CRSI), equipping harness optimizers with a Self-Improvement Memory that records the rationale and outcomes of past harness edits grouped by recurring problem. In cross-model and cross-benchmark tests, SIM beat harness-only continuation by an average of 2.36 points, with the optimized agent reaching 84.75 on workplace tasks.

"As harness engineering becomes central to agent performance, methods that let harness optimizations transfer across policy models and tasks address a key limitation of today's one-shot optimization approaches."

Developer Tools & Infrastructure

Cognition Launches Devin Bots: Persistent Agent Teams That Merge 4.5x More PRs

Cognition introduced Devin Bots, coordinator agents that break down work and spin up parallel Devins on individual cloud machines to build, test, and improve software around the clock. Bots integrate via Slack, voice, and scheduled triggers, monitor CI health and Linear blockers, and Cognition reports engineers merging 4.5x more PRs with the multi-agent setup.

"Devin Bots represents a shift from single-session coding agents to persistent multi-agent teams with explicit orchestration — the productionization of agent coordination that most labs are still demoing."

Sellbird: An Open-Source Cold Email Agent That Runs on Your Claude or ChatGPT Subscription

Sellbird is an open-source outreach pipeline that runs end-to-end on a Claude Code or Codex CLI subscription with no API keys or per-token billing. It discovers prospects, reads buying signals from their websites, verifies emails, drafts personalized sequences, and holds all outbound mail for human approval by default.

"A notable example of agents leveraging consumer subscriptions as compute — cheap, self-hosted automation that also previews the token-fraud and terms-of-service tensions brewing around subscription-based agent usage."

Industry & Applications

Signal Blocks OS-Level AI Agents From Reading Your Screen

Signal is restricting screenshots on iOS and Android to prevent agents like Gemini and Siri from reading users' messages, calling OS-integrated agentic AI 'hostile to privacy' and an 'existential threat to privacy at the application layer.' President Meredith Whittaker urged Apple, Google, and Microsoft to give developers proper tools to restrict agent data access, admitting the screenshot block is an incomplete remedy.

"The first major open clash between an encrypted app and OS-native agents marks agents as a genuine attack surface — and foreshadows a broader developer revolt over agent access to application data."

Apple Is Hiring Engineers to Build Its Own General-Purpose AI Agent

A job listing spotted by Bloomberg reveals Apple is building an agentic AI assistant that can browse the web, work across apps, write code, call services, and 'coordinate specialized sub-agents.' The role spans Siri, UI Frameworks, and Apple Foundation Models teams, with iOS 27.3 also opening Siri AI integration to all third-party apps.

"Apple entering the general-purpose agent race — with explicit multi-agent coordination ambitions — would bring agent computing to billions of devices and intensify the exact privacy tensions Signal is protesting."

Anthropic made its Nasdaq debut under ticker ANT after raising $81.3 billion, beating SpaceX for the most valuable IPO ever and opening 34% above its $83 offer price. The listing sets a precedent for other public AI labs but puts the company's safety-first reputation under new shareholder scrutiny.

More Than Half of All Work in Linear Is Now Created by Agents

Linear COO Cristina Cordova revealed that over 50% of issues created in Linear come from agents, up from 3% a year ago, as the company passed $100M ARR with 40,000+ customers. She argues SaaS products that settle for being 'a database behind an MCP server' will be commoditized, and that agent usage and app usage must grow together.

"One of the clearest production data points yet that agentic workflows — not human ticket-filing — are becoming the default way work enters development systems, with big implications for SaaS business models."

Token Fraud Ecosystem Forces ChatPRD to Kill Free Trials

Claire Vo of ChatPRD documented a flood of trial abuse, including organized groups looping free trials and promo codes to tunnel tokens for resale or to run commercial operations — even an international Roblox and Fortnite map studio running on the product's agent. She is shutting down free trials entirely, a pattern retailers and other agent-facing services are increasingly echoing.

"As agents become a distribution channel, they also become an attack surface for economic abuse — and the death of free trials may be an early casualty of the agentic era."
