Google Returns to the Frontier: Gemini 4 Argon Ships With 1M-Token Output — But Only for Trusted Testers

Google DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Argon, claiming state-of-the-art on 13 of 19 benchmarks and an industry-leading 1M-token output limit via Long Decode Continuation. Access is initially restricted to government users and cyber defenders in the Fairwind Program while Google hardens guardrails, with pricing at $2/$10 per million tokens during the intro period. Internal deployments already include Argon agents migrating 800K+ lines of kernel C/C++ to Rust and freeing 300 TiB of data-center memory.

"A 1M-token output ceiling fundamentally changes long-horizon agentic workflows, and Google's guardrail-first phased release is becoming the template for shipping frontier cyber-capable models."

Context Language Models: Meta Moves Context Management From the Harness Into the Weights

New research introduces CLMs, which treat context as an editable file the model updates itself, rather than an append-only log managed by external harness logic. Zero-shot CLMs beat state-of-the-art context-management strategies — 11.4% higher accuracy with 21.5% fewer FLOPs on BrowseComp-Plus and 65% greater improvement on a 24-hour multi-agent repo task. An online RL method improves performance 47.6% while cutting compute.

"This is a paradigm shift for context engineering: if models learn to manage their own context, the entire industry of external compaction and memory harnesses could be absorbed into the model itself."

AlphaGo Alum: LLMs Still Don't Reason — And Chain-of-Thought Isn't Fixing It

Thore Graepel, a core AlphaGo team member who left DeepMind, argues that chain-of-thought is still System 1 next-token prediction iterated longer, not genuine deliberation — lacking persistent epistemic state, separated knowledge from reasoning, and often fabricated after the fact. He proposes architectures with explicit belief ledgers and evidence-enforced updates, akin to AlphaGo's game-tree search.

"As agents take on high-stakes scientific and medical work, the gap between plausible-sounding reasoning and auditable reasoning becomes a safety and reliability problem, not just an academic one."

Superhuman AI Conquers Stratego, the Imperfect-Information Game That Stumped Machines for Decades

A Nature paper presents the first superhuman Stratego AI, achieved with reinforcement learning, test-time compute, and a second neural network that guesses the identity of hidden opponent pieces. Notably, the system was built on a modest budget, challenging the assumption that game breakthroughs require massive compute.

"Reasoning under hidden information is far closer to real-world decision-making than chess or Go, making this a meaningful marker on the path to general strategic agents."

Judge Tosses Chegg and Penske Antitrust Suits Over Google's AI Overviews

US District Judge Amit Mehta dismissed lawsuits from Chegg and Penske Media alleging that Google's AI-powered search features illegally diverted web traffic, ruling that publishers' 'expectation' of referral traffic is not a legal agreement. The decision is a major setback for publishers seeking legal remedies against AI summarization of their content.

"With antitrust law closed off as a remedy, publishers and AI companies will have to negotiate the economics of content in the AI era through licensing deals or new regulation instead."
