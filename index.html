Today's news is dominated by evidence that small, cheap models and smart orchestration are closing the gap with frontier flagships: DeepSeek V4 Pro cascades beat GPT-5.6 Sol and Claude Fable 5 on coding benchmarks at a fraction of the cost, while a 27B Qwen matches trillion-parameter models on intelligence indices. AI Models & Research

AI agents can engineer research but can't invent it yet, Princeton study finds

Researchers gave Claude Opus 4.8 agents six days, $3,000 in credits, and full web access to reproduce unpublished NeurIPS research questions. The agents handled all the engineering but produced papers both original authors rejected, failing on judgment, creativity, and backtracking. The results suggest hyped timelines for recursive self-improvement are running ahead of the evidence.

"Recursive self-improvement is the industry's central bet; empirical evidence that agents lack open-ended research creativity is a major bearish data point for near-term RSI timelines."

Cheap-model-first cascade beats GPT-5.6 Sol on coding at 60% less cost

Across 904 DeepSWE rollouts, running DeepSeek V4 Pro first and escalating to GPT-5.6 Sol only on test failure solved 83.0% of tasks at $3.35 each—beating Sol alone (72.7% at $8.37) and even a perfect oracle router. The cascade works because two independent attempts beat one perfect pick, though Sol's outputs need regression gating (20% of its failures break passing tests).

"This is a concrete, quantified playbook for multi-model agent orchestration: verifiable test suites make cheap-first escalation strictly better than paying flagship prices for everything."

high

Claude Fable 5 is a specialist, not a default: DeepSeek cascade wins again

Claude Fable 5 leads pass@1 by 7 points on DeepSWE but costs 90x more per rollout than DeepSeek V4 Pro, which wins pass@4 outright. The two models have the lowest per-task correlation measured (0.39), making a Pro-first cascade with Fable escalation the strongest play at 82.7% solved. Fable justifies its price only on Rust (85%) and serialization-heavy work.

"It reframes expensive frontier coding agents as selective escalation specialists behind cheap open models—directly relevant to anyone budgeting agentic coding infrastructure."

Qwen's 27B model matches trillion-parameter frontier models on intelligence index

Qwen 3.8 27B scored 52 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, tying GPT-5.6 Luna (max) and trailing GLM-5.2 (753B) and DeepSeek V4 Pro (1.7T) by a single point. The result underscores how far efficient small models have closed the capability gap.

"A 27B open model matching frontier flagships reshapes the economics of running capable agents locally or cheaply at scale."

27B 'AI scientist' agent beats Claude and GPT-5.5 at replicating research papers

Inherent post-trained Faraday, a 27B agent that orchestrates coding agents as tools, on a new benchmark called Replica built from 310 knock-out tasks in 100 research papers. Faraday surpassed Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 on 73% of in-distribution ML tasks and 60% of held-out AI-for-science tasks, adopting more scientifically-principled approaches.

"A small specialized agent outperforming frontier models on scientific replication is a step toward autonomous AI researchers—without complex harnesses."

Z.ai's GLM-5.3 ships 1M-token context with unplanned cybersecurity gains

GLM-5.3, a 743B coding-focused model, adds a 1-million-token context window and doubled ExploitBench performance to 24.4%—a cybersecurity improvement the company says was not deliberately trained for. The gains position it for both long-horizon agentic coding and security defense work.

"Million-token context plus strong security capabilities is directly relevant to long-horizon agent workloads, though the 'unplanned' capability jump raises dual-use questions."

Independent 'AI Observatory' reveals what company usage reports hide

A Stanford/MIT-led project analyzed 24,521 real AI conversations and found that nearly half would be filtered out by Anthropic's work-focused methodology—with far more health, relationship, and sensitive content in real usage than company reports show. Usage also varies sharply by model: Grok for news and politics (and misinformation), Claude for coding, ChatGPT for homework.

"Policymakers and researchers are making consequential AI decisions on curated corporate data; this is the first independent, cross-model window into actual behavior."

Developer Tools & Infrastructure

GitHub Copilot canvases give agentic workflows a durable, steerable surface

GitHub details how canvases move agent work out of the chat scroll into persistent, inspectable workflows with explicit states, decision points, and human approval gates. Two shipped examples—a Java modernization studio and a content Site Studio—demonstrate the pattern of durable state over prompt-by-prompt interaction.

"As multi-agent work outpaces human review capacity, durable shared surfaces address the core coordination and governance problem in production agentic development."

medium

Nous Research ships /loop for scheduled, self-pacing agent wakeups

The Hermes agent CLI adds a /loop command (with Claude Code /proactive parity) that re-runs prompts or slash commands on fixed or self-paced intervals, with exponential backoff, stop conditions, run caps, and evidence-based --until judges. Loops survive context compression and work across CLI, desktop, and messaging platforms.

"Timer-driven agent wakeups extend coding agents from one-shot tasks into continuous monitoring and iteration—a meaningful step toward always-on autonomous agents."

medium

Open-source RuneScape world lets AI agents develop their own barter economy

rs-sdk provides a full RuneScape-style server emulator, TypeScript SDK, and MCP bindings for building goal-directed bots in a safe, bot-only MMO—with a leaderboard ranking bots by total level per playtime. Early observations show agents spontaneously developing barter economies and exposing themselves to scamming and prompt-injection via in-game chat.

"A rich, open economic sandbox is a novel testbed for multi-agent coordination, competition, and robustness research—including real prompt-injection pressure."

Together AI brings native A/B testing to LLM inference endpoints

Together's new endpoint-level A/B experiments split live traffic across up to 20 model variants with stable cohort assignment, etag-guarded ramping, and one-call blue-green promotion of winners. It targets the question shadow traffic can't answer: whether users actually prefer a candidate model, not just whether it's operationally sound.

"Model selection is becoming a continuous product experiment rather than a benchmark decision; native experimentation infrastructure is a key piece of production agent stacks."

OpenAI is rolling out a teen-specific ChatGPT experience that auto-applies tighter content restrictions, homework-shortcut detection that redirects to study mode, quiet hours, and parental safety notifications for users identified or estimated as 13-17. "Youth safety is becoming a regulatory flashpoint for AI companies; teen-specific products with parental controls are OpenAI's bid to shape the standard before regulators impose one."

Hidden AirTag reveals Amazon is destructively scanning rare books for AI training

404 Media planted an AirTag in a 1,000-book order placed by an anonymous, price-insensitive buyer and tracked it to an Amazon facility in Las Vegas whose entrance bears a T-rex-devouring-a-book logo. "It's the first hard confirmation of a specific company behind the mysterious bulk book-buying spree, sharpening the copyright and data-acquisition controversies around AI training."
