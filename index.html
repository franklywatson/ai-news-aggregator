AI Models & Research

OpenAI's 10,000-Agent Swarm Claims a Millennium Prize — and a Fight Over Credit

OpenAI says an internal model, run across roughly 10,000 concurrent agents over 88 hours, produced a solution to the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, verified in Lean. The milestone is overshadowed by accusations from NYU's Tristan Buckmaster that OpenAI leveraged his and Anthropic's Levent Alpöge's year-long AI-assisted work without credit, which OpenAI denies. The company says it will not claim the $1 million prize.

"It's a landmark demonstration of massive-scale multi-agent orchestration solving a 90-year-old problem — but the credit dispute raises serious questions about whether frontier labs' user data practices can effectively scoop confidential research."

Simon Willison: A Rumor Is Now Enough to Trigger Millions in Agent Spending

Willison unpacks the OpenAI Navier-Stokes timeline, noting the effort began days after rumors circulated that the problem had been solved, and that OpenAI 'cannot rule out' that de-identified user data helped train the model. He draws a parallel to security research, where mere rumors of vulnerabilities now trigger automated agent-driven exploitation. He also flags the unresolved ambiguity of what 'data used to improve model performance' actually means.

"This reframes the incident as a systemic risk: any hint of a breakthrough may now trigger brute-force agent swarms, changing incentives around open research and confidential tool usage."

GPT-6 Astra, Looped Transformers, and the Hidden Reasoning Debate

Sebastian Raschka analyzes reports that GPT-6 Astra uses looped transformer / recurrent depth architectures, explaining the mechanism and reviewing new research showing compute-matched looped models need 6.8–18% less training compute. He argues looping is unlikely to be the cause of Astra's reduced reasoning-trace monitorability — a concern OpenAI's chief scientist acknowledged is trending negatively for other reasons. The piece also documents Astra's leap in computer-use capabilities.

"It cuts through confused reporting on chain-of-thought monitorability while surfacing a concrete architectural trend — recurrent depth — that frontier labs appear to be adopting for efficiency, with direct implications for interpretability."

Terence Tao: AI Is Mining Open Math Problems 'Non-Renewably'

Tao warns that even the rumor of someone working on a problem can now trigger massive AI-powered efforts to solve it first, and that incentives are shifting toward not sharing promising research directions at all. He argues this could reverse centuries of open-science tradition and do serious long-term damage to mathematics.

"One of the world's leading mathematicians is publicly describing a structural threat to open science posed by agentic AI — a signal that AI's impact on research culture is arriving faster than expected."

OECD: Students Who Use AI Score Worse — Unless Taught to Question It

The first PISA study since AI went mainstream, covering 760,000 students in 91 countries, found AI users generally underperform non-users in science after controlling for socioeconomic status. But students trained to critically assess AI outputs and using AI weekly for general learning outperformed non-users, suggesting intentional use is the deciding factor.

"It's the largest evidence base yet on AI's real-world cognitive effects, and it lands on a nuanced answer: harm comes from offloading productive struggle, not from the tool itself."

Microsoft Patches Record 972 Bugs as Industry Braces for AI-Assisted Attacks

Microsoft's September release fixed a record ~972 vulnerabilities, 112 critical, more than double last year's pace, following an open letter from over 100 AI companies warning of a narrowing window before AI-enabled attack tools mass-exploit unpatched flaws. Researchers call the patch surge the 'new normal' even as active exploits haven't yet spiked.

"Agentic AI is reshaping the offensive-defensive balance in cybersecurity, forcing an unprecedented industrialization of vulnerability patching across the software ecosystem."
